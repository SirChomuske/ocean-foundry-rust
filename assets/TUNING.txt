Ocean Foundry - generator tuning (development build)
====================================================

ocean-tuning.json, in this folder, holds the internal numbers of the ocean
generator. They are not ordinary settings: they are the knobs of the method
itself, kept in a file while the method is still being worked on, so it can
be adjusted without a new build of the program.

How to use it
-------------
  1. Open ocean-tuning.json in any text editor (the app has a button for it:
     Advanced > Generator tuning > Open tuning file).
  2. Change a number and save the file.
  3. With "Live" on, the app bakes again by itself within a second.
     Otherwise press Bake.

  - Delete the file (or press "Restore defaults") to get the defaults back.
    "Restore defaults" keeps the numbers it replaces in
    ocean-tuning.previous.json.
  - A part you leave out of the file uses its default. A file holding only
    { "_version": 4, "reflection": { "cliff": 0 } } changes that one number.
  - If the file cannot be read (a missing comma, a misspelt name), the app
    says what is wrong and on which line, and keeps the previous build.
  - Names must stay exactly as they are; only change the values.
  - The "_about" line is a note and is ignored.
  - "_version" says which method the numbers were written for (now 4). When
    the method changes so much that old numbers no longer mean the same, the
    app sets an older file aside as ocean-tuning.previous.json, writes a
    fresh one and says so.

Lengths are in blocks unless they say otherwise. "Main wave" means a wave as
long as the "Peak distance" setting.


waves - the open sea
--------------------
The sea is a wave spectrum (JONSWAP) sampled as many small waves and added up.

components          How many waves the sea is the sum of. More is smoother
                    and never repeats, and costs bake time.
peak_enhancement    How sharply the spectrum peaks at the main waves: 1 is a
                    fully developed sea, 3.3 the measured average, 7 very
                    peaked (a clean swell).
narrowing_at_calm   Extra narrowing of the peak when "Irregularity" is 0, so
                    a regular swell is nearly one wavelength.
spread_at_calm      How narrowly the wave directions are bunched at
spread_at_wild      Irregularity 0 and at 1. Larger is narrower: 300 gives
                    long straight crests, 4 a confused sea.
tail_from           The short waves "Swell detail" adds start at this
                    multiple of the main wave frequency.
tail_energy         Share of the sea's energy in them at full Swell detail.
tail_components     Share of the components spent on them.
shortest_length     Waves shorter than this cannot be built from blocks and
                    are left out.
height_in_deviations  What "Wave height" measures: 4 is the average of the
                    highest third of the waves, 5.1 of the highest tenth.
                    Smaller makes a taller sea for the same setting.
tone_smoothing      The colour of the water follows the sea with its short
tone_smoothing_least  waves smoothed away, so it shows the swell and not
                    every wavelet (this is what keeps the colour from looking
                    grainy). The first is a share of the peak distance, the
                    second the least smoothing in blocks. 0 colours every
                    wavelet.
chop_most           How far full "Crest sharpness" pulls the water toward the
                    crests: 1 is a true trochoid.
cusp_limit          Sharpening stops before the steepest crests fold over.
wander              How far the crests wander from the straight lines that a
                    sum of plane waves would make, as a share of the peak
                    distance. A sea drawn without it reads as a pattern: long,
                    straight, evenly spaced ridges. 0 turns it off.
wander_size         Over how many peak distances a bend comes and goes. Small
                    numbers give a close, choppy wobble; large ones let whole
                    stretches of sea run at their own angle.


near_land - what land does to the waves (only with land)
--------------------------------------------------------
breaker_ratio       The tallest wave the water can carry, as a share of its
                    depth. Measured values run from 0.6 to 0.8.
height_at_shore     Share of their height waves keep at the waterline.
depth_smoothing     Blocks over which the depth is averaged before the waves
                    feel it.
least_depth         Waves are slowed as if the water were never shallower.
slowest             The most the crests can bunch up as they slow: 3 lets
                    them come three times closer together.
shelter_rays        Shelter behind land is found by looking upwind along this
shelter_fan         many lines, fanned out by this many degrees to each side,
shelter_reach       up to this many main waves away.
bend_share          Share of the waves that gets round corners into shelter.
shelter_floor       Share of their height long waves keep in full shelter.
chop_regrow         Short waves grow back behind land over this many blocks.


reflection - waves thrown back by the shore (only with land)
------------------------------------------------------------
The shore is read from the shape of the land: a low sandy slope with shallow
water in front is a beach, land that stands steep and tall from the water is
a cliff, the rest is rocky shore. In front of a shore that throws waves back,
the sea is the waves arriving and the same waves leaving, crossing.

cliff               Share of a wave's height a cliff throws back.
rocky               The same for a rocky shore.
beach               The same for a beach (waves break there and are spent).
reach               How far out the returning waves are seen, in main waves:
                    at this distance about a third is left. Waves spread
                    and lose height on the way, so this is short: a quarter
                    of a main wave, not the whole sea.
soften              How many blocks wide a square the returning wave is
                    spread over before it is added. A shore far off sends
                    its wave home with crests closer together than a block,
                    so read outright it draws a crease of light across the
                    waves wherever the two trains fall out of step. 0 adds
                    it as it stands.


ripples - fine ripples that exist only as shading
-------------------------------------------------
slope               Slope the ripples add at full "Ripple shading".
size                Their size in blocks: the way the waves run (short), and
                    along the crests (longer). They are small waves of the
                    same wind, so they lie the same way as the swell.
sideways            How much of that slope they also have along the crests.
pattern_share       How much of that slope a library or image ripple pattern
                    gives at "Pattern strength" 1. Default 0.4.
seabed_break_depth  With the imagined sea floor, waves break more readily
                    in water shallower than this many wave heights.
                    Default 1.3.


shore_foam - white water where the sea meets land
-------------------------------------------------
sand_reach          How many blocks out from a beach the foam reaches...
sand_strength       ...and how strong it is there (0 to 1).
rock_reach          The same on a rocky shore, where it is most broken up.
rock_strength
cliff_reach         The same at the foot of a cliff.
cliff_strength
height_sway         How much the height of the arriving waves widens the
                    foam: at 0.4 from 0.6 times in a calm to 1.4 in a storm.
channel_share       Share of the foam left on the banks of a river channel.
sheltered           Share of the foam a completely sheltered shore keeps.
patch_size          Size of the patches the foam breaks into.
fall_reach          White water where a waterfall lands in the sea: blocks
fall_reach_most     it reaches for each block the water falls, the most it
fall_strength       reaches, and how white it is at the foot of the fall.


outflow - a river running out into the sea
------------------------------------------
Waves meeting a river's current are slowed, stand taller and closer together,
and the shortest are stopped; the river's water shows as a lighter plume.

speed               How fast the water leaves a river mouth, as a share of
speed_for_length    the speed of waves this many blocks long. 0: rivers do
                    nothing to the waves.
spread              How quickly the outflow widens: blocks to each side for
                    every block it travels.
reach               It fades away over this many widths of its mouth.
gain_most           The most a wave grows running against it.
fall_share          Share of that speed the water keeps below a waterfall.
plume_tint          How much the river's water lightens the sea it enters.


flow - a current running past land ("Current speed" above 0, with land)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
The current is the same everywhere, so open water shows nothing of it. Land
in its way leaves slack water behind it, has water piled against the face
that meets the current, and starts a wake of standing waves. Heights here
are shares of the head of the current: its speed squared over twice gravity,
in blocks (so it grows with "Current speed" and with smaller blocks).

slack_most          Share of the current that is lost right behind land.
slack_spread        How quickly the slack water mixes with the current at
                    its sides, 0 to 0.33. More closes the slack sooner.
slack_widths        The current is back about this many widths of the land
                    behind it: a wide obstacle leaves a long wake.
pile_reach          How far in front of land the water is piled up, blocks.
pile_height         How high it is piled against the land, in heads.
lee_drop            How far it is drawn down behind the land, in heads.
wave_height         Height of the standing waves behind land, in heads.
divergent           Height of the crests that fan out to the sides, against
                    the crests that lie across the current.
wave_reach          The standing waves are lost in the sea over about this
                    many distances between their crests. That distance grows
                    with the square of the speed, so a faster current leaves
                    a longer wake.
sources             The shore is gathered into at most this many places
                    that each start a wake. More follows the shape of the
                    land more closely and costs bake time.
shortest            Standing waves shorter than the first number of blocks
                    are left out; from the second they have full height.
lift_most           The water is never lifted or lowered by more than this
                    many heads.
calm                How much of their height short waves lose in slack water.
foam                How much white water the current leaves where it strikes
                    land and in the slack water behind it.
foam_life           How many seconds foam lasts. It is carried along
                    meanwhile, so it reaches as far as the current runs in
                    this time: farther than the waves.
foam_streak         Length and width of the streaks that foam is drawn into,
                    in blocks.
foam_from           A current slower than this many metres a second leaves
                    no foam; at three times this it leaves all of it.
impact_bonus        How much the speed of the current raises the white water
                    at a shore it strikes, on top of what the shore gives on
                    its own. Was 0.35 before it flooded a fast current's
                    shores.
fast_cap            How much white water a fast current is rid of at the
                    faces it strikes, as it reaches three times foam_from. A
                    fast current already breaks the sea white all around it,
                    so those faces must not read as a sheet painted on top of
                    that. The trail it drags keeps its own cap: that white
                    wash is how a fast current reads.
squeeze             How much faster the current runs where two shores press
                    it together, as a share of its open speed. The same water
                    through a smaller gap goes faster; 0 leaves it even.
squeeze_reach       How far to the side the room is measured, in blocks.
                    Shores closer together than twice this squeeze the
                    current between them.
foam_stretch        How far a squeezed current draws the foam out, against
                    how much faster that current runs. 0 leaves it where it
                    was made.
stagnation          How much the current is stopped where it runs into a face
                    that leaves it no way on, so the water piles up there and
                    goes aside. 0 lets it run on as if the face were not there.
mix                 How much of the foam a wake carries is stirred sideways
                    into the water beside it: water behind an obstacle mixes
                    out into the stream, so the wake widens downwind. 0 keeps
                    the wake the width of what made it.
mix_spread          How many blocks to each side that stirring reaches.
squeeze_lift        How high the water stands where two shores squeeze the
                    stream, as a share of the head the current has. A narrow
                    way runs the water faster and lifts it a little.


whitecaps - where waves break, and the foam they leave
------------------------------------------------------
breaking_steepness  A crest breaks where the water is this steep. Seas built
                    from blocks are steeper than real ones, so this is above
                    the 0.3 to 0.44 of real waves.
most_breaking       However steep the sea, no more than this share of it
                    breaks at one moment. With the foam left behind, about
                    four times as much shows white.
softness            Range of steepness over which breaking sets in.
short_wave_share    How much short waves count toward the steepness. At 1
                    every ripple breaks, at 0 only the main waves.
foam_amount_sway    How far "Foam amount" moves the threshold.
trail_time          How long foam lasts, in main wave periods.
trail_samples       Past moments looked at to find that foam. More is
                    smoother and costs bake time.
trail_strength      How white a passing breaker leaves the water behind it.
trail_most          Foam left behind is never a solid sheet: at most this
                    share of its blocks turn white.
surf_ease           How much more easily waves break where the water is too
                    shallow for them.


spent_foam - extra white water on tall waves ("Storm foam")
-----------------------------------------------------------
Nothing here does anything while "Storm foam" is 0, which is the standard.
cover_per_height    How much of the sea is white in all, by the height of
cover_power         the waves: cover_per_height times the height to the
cover_most          power cover_power, never above cover_most. The standard
                    numbers follow the whitecap cover measured at sea: 1% at
                    2.5 m, 6% at 7 m, 17% at 13 m, with "Foam amount" and
                    "Storm foam" both at 0.5.
breaking_share      Share of that cover that is crests breaking right now.
                    The rest is foam they left behind.
steepness_floor     Waves less steep than this never break, however large
                    the sea.
life                How long the lace lasts, and how long the streaks last,
                    in main wave periods.
samples             Past moments looked at for each. More is smoother and
                    costs bake time.
step                The foam left is worked out every this many blocks and
                    filled in between.
lace_share          Share of the foam left that lies as lace; the rest is
                    streaks.
lace_size           Size of the meshes of the lace along the waves' path and
                    across it, in blocks.
lace_width          How wide the threads of the lace are (0 to 1).
lace_from           How thin foam may be and still show as threads (0 to 1).
streak_size         Length and width of the streaks, in blocks.
streak_width        How gradually the streaks fill in as foam thickens.
streak_from         How thin foam may be and still show as streaks (0 to 1).
streak_drawn        How far along the wind foam is drawn out, in blocks.
streak_waver        How far the streaks waver from side to side, in blocks.
streak_even         How evenly the streaks lie over the sea: 0 only where
                    waves broke lately, 1 the same everywhere.
most                The most white this foam can be, as a share of
                    colour.foam_most.
edge                How gradually a block turns white.
gain_most           The foam left is never multiplied up by more than this
                    to reach the cover, so a sea that hardly breaks stays
                    clean.


colour
------
foam_white          No longer read. The colour of white water is the
                    "Foam colour" setting in the app.
foam_gain           How strongly "Foam amount" turns water white.
foam_most           The most white allowed (1 = pure foam colour).
foam_weight         Extra say white water gets when the block types are
                    chosen.

How the colour of a column is found. The water runs between two chosen
colours, the "Dark colour" and the "Light colour", and every column has a
tone between them: 0 is the dark colour, 2 the light colour. Level water in
full sun is at 1, half way (with "Level water" at one half); with less
"Sunlight" everything moves toward the dark colour. Either colour may be
anything, black included: nothing here keeps the sea from going black if
that is what is chosen. Two things move the tone, and "Wave shading" scales
both:

slope_light         The direction of the light, which leads: a face turned to
                    the sun is lighter, one turned away darker. Raise it for
                    more relief; at 0 the direction of the sun no longer
                    shades the waves.
crest_light         The thickness of the water, which supports: light comes
                    through the thin water of a crest, a trough is deep.
                    Raise it for paler crests and darker troughs wherever
                    they face. Too much and the colour only shows the height.
crest_span          The height above the middle of the swell at which a crest
                    counts as a full one: so many times the usual rise and
                    fall of the sea in hand, and never less than so many
                    blocks (so a nearly flat sea is nearly one colour).
tone_reach          How far the tone can go: the darkest face falls to this
                    share of level water; the lightest rises this far above.
                    Raise the second for sunlit faces nearer the light colour.
patch_tone          The wind does not ruffle the sea evenly: how much darker
patch_size          and lighter broad patches are, and their size in blocks
                    along the wind and across it.
sky_shade           Without sun (overcast, or in shadow) the light comes from
sky_lift            the whole sky: crests stand open to it, troughs less.
                    sky_shade is how strong that is, sky_lift how quickly it
                    changes with height.
bed_tint            How strongly the sea floor tints shallow water at full
                    "Water clarity".
darkest             The share of light left at "Daylight" 0. At 0 the water
                    is black there; raise it (0.55, say) to keep some light
                    however low "Daylight" is set.
white_dims          How much of that darkening foam and reflections take.

Reflections lie over the water's colour as a layer of their own.

reflection_white    With "Reflections take the light colour" on, reflections
                    are the light colour made paler: this far from it toward
                    white. 0: they are the light colour itself.
reflection_tint     How much of the colour left after that they keep. 1: all
                    of it. 0: a grey as pale as reflection_white makes. With
                    the default colours the two numbers give #d9ecf5, the
                    pale sky the reflections had before.
glint_share         Strength of the glint of the sun, and of the sheen of the
sheen_share         sky on faces seen at a low angle.
glint_spread        How far a slope may be from the one that mirrors the sun
                    and still glint: at "Glint spread" 0, and at 1. Fine
                    ripples break a narrow glint into sparkles.
sky_ratio           How bright the sky is against the light from within the
                    water; sets how quickly the sheen grows at low angles.
sheen_deepen        How much a face turned toward the onlooker may deepen
                    below the colour of level water.
bright_depth        With two bright colours the sea has little room to go
bright_from         darker and looks pale. Faces turned from the sun then
                    go this much darker and more colourful than the dark
                    colour itself. bright_from is the lightness of the dark
                    colour (0 black, 1 white) at which this starts and at
                    which it is in full. 0: never.

Choosing the blocks.

hue_care            How much more a wrong hue counts than a wrong lightness
                    when a block is matched to a colour.
washed_care         How much more again it counts when the blocks have less
                    colour than the water was given: grey blocks under glass
                    look washed out. 0: only the nearness counts.
care_from           hue_care and washed_care count in full only for water
                    with at least the second of these as its strength of
                    colour (0 grey, a clear sea blue about 0.1), and not at
                    all below the first: grey storm water has no hue to
                    keep, and there the nearest blocks are simply the
                    nearest.
texture_most        The most uneven a block's face may be to be used, as it
                    shows through the glass over it. Higher lets in more
                    textured blocks.
glass_apart         How much it counts against a pair of blocks that the
                    glass is of another colour than the two look together
                    from above. Blue glass over green looks teal on the map
                    and blue along every step in the waves. 0: only the look
                    from above counts.
block_gain          A further block type is taken only if it improves the
                    match by at least this share.
dither_reach        Dithering mixes the two nearest tones in use; how far
dither_most         between them it reaches at full "Dithering", and the
                    largest step in colour it will bridge.
dither_patches      A pattern that changes from block to block looks grainy.
dither_patch_size   dither_patches is the share of the mixing done instead in
                    patches a few blocks across, drawn out along the crests;
                    dither_patch_size is their length and width in blocks.
                    0: the fine block-by-block pattern only.
lone_blocks         0: a block whose colour none of the four beside it share
                    takes the nearest colour among theirs, so no single
                    blocks stand out. 1: lone blocks are kept. Foam is a
                    scatter of white blocks and is never touched.
layer_blend         With two or three layers of glass, a border between two
layer_reach         block colours is blended through the layers: within
                    layer_reach blocks of it each layer changes colour along
                    a line of its own, so one hard border becomes several
                    faint ones. Open water keeps one clean colour.
                    layer_blend 0: off; the layers of a column may then
                    simply differ in colour wherever that matches better.
layer_smooth        How far, in blocks, the colours are softened before the
                    middle and the top layer are chosen. The top layer runs
                    smoothest.
layer_dither        How strongly the lowest, the middle and the top layer
                    are dithered along a border, as a multiple of
                    "Dithering". The glass above softens the pattern, so
                    the lowest layer takes the most and the top none.

Foam.

foam_grain          Foam is a scatter of white blocks. Thresholds run from
foam_edge           foam_grain to 1; foam_edge is how gradually a block
                    turns white.
foam_streaks        Share of the scatter that follows streaks drawn out along
foam_streak_size    the waves' path, and their length and width in blocks.
aerated_tint        How much the churned water around foam is lightened.
shade_slope         The steepness of sea the shading is laid out for (the
                    typical slope of its swell: about 0.06 in a calm, 0.27 in
                    a moderate sea). In a steeper sea the shading is eased by
                    as much, so a storm does not become a hard pattern of the
                    darkest and the lightest blocks. 0: never eased.


Not in this file yet
--------------------
The numbers by which the shore is read (how steep and tall a cliff is, how
low a beach) and by which a river builds its delta are still fixed in the
program (survey.rs and terrain.rs). Say so if you want them here.
